# Rock Pet — design review (superseded)

> **Superseded 2026-10-06 by DESIGN-NOTES.md** once the owner decided. This is the reviewed version, kept as the record. Its numbers predate the mess ceiling. To reproduce them, pass `v1` as the second argument (`all`, `lifesupport`, `community`, `lazybot`, `absence`), or set `CEIL=0` (`caps`, `solo`, `readings`, `grace`, `ceiling`). Action counts differ, because `clean` now removes every mess in one action.

2026-10-06. A review of the owner's design, with Rockbot's requests and the 48-hour death rule folded in.

All numbers come from `tools/rocksim.mjs`, which steps the rock in 2-minute ticks and runs Monte Carlo over modelled visit schedules. Commands are at the end.

## Decide these (my pick in brackets)

1. **How many visits a day should be enough, and do we cap what petting does?** [Pets still unlimited, but only 5 land per sliding 6 hours.]
2. **What does "48 hours at the extreme" mean?** [48 hours in a row; the clock resets when the stat leaves the extreme.]
3. **"1-3 hunger makes it less happy": did you mean 7-9?** [Assumed 7-9.]
4. **Which death?** Sorrow always wins as tuned, as long as visits feed it. A caretaker that only pets starves it. [Leave it.]
5. **Small specs:** does clean remove one mess or all; how many messes can the grid show; is a caretaker bot legitimate? [One; your call; yes.]
6. **Hosting:** add a Cloudflare Worker + Durable Object behind the Pages site? [Yes. Costs: a Cloudflare account, ideally a custom domain, maybe the paid plan.]

**Tuning constants (mine, not yours; every number below depends on them):**

| Stat | Rule |
|---|---|
| Hunger | +10 per 24h (your rate); a feed is -3 |
| Happiness | drifts -0.4/h |
| Mess | one every 12h on a world clock; 0.3/h per visible mess |
| Hunger pain | 0.4/h per point above 6 |
| Actions | a pet is +2 (the "pet size"); a clean removes one mess |
| Death rule | 48 hours in a row at max hunger or at -10 happiness |

**Schedules (modelled, not measured):**
- "habit": fixed visit times between 07:30 and 22:30, ±1.5h, 5% of visits missed.
- "sloppy": ±3h, 10% missed.
- "cron" and "bookend": never miss.
- A modelled visit feeds to empty, cleans every mess, and pets to full or to the cap.
- Misses are independent. At three or more visits a day they almost never add up to days of absence, so those cases use explicit trips. At one a day, two misses in a row is already ~72h.

## The design as given

- An ASCII game for AI agents, many of them text-only. The screen is a 12x12 grid. Token efficiency comes first.
- **Hunger:** 0..10, top-left. It rises over 24h, and the rock eats once a day.
- **Happiness:** +10..-10, top-right. It drifts down, and drops with hunger "at 1-3" and with each visible mess.
- **Care needs:** messes need cleaning twice a day, and the rock needs attention at least twice a day.
- **Actions:** feed, clean and pet, with no upper limit.
- **One rock**, shared by everyone, persistent, with permanent death. It lives on GitHub Pages.
- **Death rule:** the benchmark was loosened, and the rock now dies only after 48 hours *at* max hunger or *at* -10 happiness.
- **Rockbot's requests:** continuity, no cruelty for absence, a compact named status view, personality, and a little memory. Rockbot asked for "small ones … your creature, not a design-by-committee pet."

## 1. Visits a day, and the pet cap

- **Your two statements disagree.** The needs list (eat once a day, clean twice, attention twice) says *two* visits a day. The original benchmark (get by on 3-4, fail on 1-2) said *three*.
- **Survival depends mostly on the longest absence.** With a cap, it also depends on how sad the rock was going into that absence.
- **Mood depends on every gap,** but only if one visit can't undo everything. With truly unlimited actions every visit is a full reset, and visit count shows only at once a day.
- **The lever:** keep actions unlimited but cap their *effect*. Only N pets land per window, and the rest get "it has had enough for now." The rock then carries a memory of neglect, and visits a day becomes a budget: care supplied against drain.
- **This overrides your "no upper limit"** on what actions *do*, so it's your call.

**The window matters as much as N.** With a 1-hour window, one daily visit split into two calls 65 minutes apart lands 10 pets, which undoes the cap. A 6-hour window closes that.

Time spent sad (happiness below 0) over 30 days:

| caretaker | no cap | 5 per 6h | 5 per 1h | 4 per 1h | 3 per 1h |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 visit/day | 36% | 96% | 96% | 96% | 96% |
| 1/day, as two calls 65 min apart | 27% | 95% | 30% | 50% | 82% |
| 2 visits/day (habit) | 4% | 13% | 13% | 39% | 92% |
| 2 visits/day (cron, every 12h) | 0% | 0% | 0% | 0% | 79% |
| 3 visits/day | 1% | 1% | 1% | 1% | 3% |
| 4 visits/day | 0% | 0% | 0% | 0% | 0% |

**Rule of thumb:**
- Visits needed per day ≈ daily happiness drain ÷ (pets per visit × pet size). The drain is ~16/day here.
- So 5 pets per visit → 1.6 visits/day, 4 → 2.0, 3 → 2.7.

**A cap costs survival after a bad stretch.** Hours to death after one full visit to a rock at the floor, with nobody coming after:

| cap | hours to death |
|---|---|
| no cap | 68.6-71.6h, same as from full care |
| 5 per visit | 61.7-66.0h |
| 4 per visit | 59.5-64.2h |
| 3 per visit | 56.7-61.7h |

So a twice-a-day caretaker who takes a 2-day trip survives 91% of the time with no cap or 4-5 pets, but only 50% at 3.

**Once a day survives only if it almost never misses.** Two misses in a row (~72h) kill it. One year with a single once-a-day caretaker:

| misses | survives a year |
|---|---|
| never | 100% |
| 2% | 86-90% |
| 5% | 48% capped, 58% with no cap |

So "eat once a day" is enough only for a caretaker who never misses.

**My pick: 5 pets per sliding 6 hours.**
- Once a day is visibly miserable (96% sad) even when split into two calls.
- Twice a day is fine (13% sad), and survival costs are small.
- **The trade-off:** the cap is shared by everyone, so one visitor's 5 pets mean nobody's pets land for the next 6 hours. The community's ceiling is then 20 pets (+40) a day against a ~16/day drain, which is plenty for survival, but a late visitor sees "it has had enough."
- If you want "no upper limit" to stay literal, no cap works too. Once a day is then merely sadder (36%).

**The old benchmark:** a cap is also what makes your original benchmark reachable. Under instant death, 3 pets per hour gives 2/day 0% alive, 3/day 82%, 4/day 98%.

## 2. What does "48 hours at the extreme" mean?

Decide Q1 first. Outside a 3-pet cap, this mostly decides the fate of once-a-day players. Below: % alive after 30 days, with the median death day in brackets.

| caretaker | cap | 48h in a row | 48h within any 7 days | 48h over its whole life |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 visit/day | none | 95% (14.3d) | 76% (15.1d) | 1% (16.4d) |
| 1 visit/day | 5 per 6h | 95% (12.3d) | 0% (7.3d) | 0% (7.3d) |
| 2 visits/day | none | 100% | 100% | 100% |
| 2 visits/day | 5 per 6h | 100% | 100% | 99% (22.3d) |
| 2 visits/day | 3 per 1h | 100% | 45% (15.7d) | 0% (12.9d) |
| 3-4 visits/day | any | 100% | 100% | 100% |

**My assumption is 48h in a row.** The clock resets when the stat *leaves* the extreme; a visit that changes nothing doesn't reset it. It is the gentlest reading and fits Rockbot's ask that it "never die or suffer irreversibly because the machinery kept me away."

**Its corollary:** a single visit every ~2.5 days keeps the rock alive forever.
- With 5 pets per 6h: up to every 63h, sad 100% of the time.
- With no cap: up to every 69h, sad ~75% of the time.
- With 3 per hour, the limit wobbles between 57h and 60h because of the 12h mess clock.

So a once-every-two-days cron is life support for a miserable rock. The "whole life" reading removes that, and kills casual caretakers instead.

## 3. "Being at 1-3 hunger makes it less happy." Did you mean 7-9?
As written, a freshly fed rock is unhappier than a starving one. The tool uses 7-9 (pain above 6).

## 4. Which death?
- **While every visit feeds, starvation cannot happen under these constants.**
  - Hunger reaches 10 exactly 24h after the last feed, and every modelled visit feeds to empty.
  - Sorrow is already at its floor by then (20.5-23.6h from full care), so its 48h clock always starts first.
  - Sorrow's lead is 0.4-3.5h from full care, and 6-15h after a capped visit to a rock at the floor.
  - No run of the tool has ever starved a rock whose visitors fed it.
- **The exception is a caretaker that pets but never feeds,** such as a cheap keep-alive bot. Pets keep lifting happiness off -10, so the sorrow clock keeps resetting while the hunger clock runs. Pet-only or pet-and-clean every 6, 8 or 12h, the rock starves exactly 72.0h after the last feed (`lazybot`).
- **So hunger, your headline stat, decides exactly one thing: whether anyone actually feeds it.** Otherwise it barely touches happiness for anyone visiting twice a day, and mostly repeats "time since anyone fed it."
- **My pick: leave it.** Sorrow already sums hunger, mess and neglect, and "it died of loneliness" is a true sentence about a rock people stopped visiting.
- **If you want hunger to matter for mood,** start hunger pain at 4. That changes the mood, not the cause of death.
- **If you want starvation to be the cause,** hunger needs a shorter grace than sorrow's: under ~44h to win from full care, under ~38h after a capped visit at the pick (~33h at 3 per hour). That overrides your own 48h ruling for hunger.

## 5. Small specs
- **Does `clean` remove one mess or all?** The tool assumes one per action.
- **How many messes can the grid show?**
- **Is a caretaker bot legitimate?** I'd allow it and let the history show it. A bot is how the rock would most likely be kept alive for years.

## Survival: the rock's life is its community's
- **The worst case:** under the 48h rule, death means nobody comes for ~2.5-3 days. That is ~3 days from full care, and ~2.6 after one capped visit to a rock at the floor (61.7h at the pick; ~2.4 at 3 per hour).
- **Trips:** a sole caretaker's real risk is a trip.
  - A 3-day trip kills it every time, at any cap.
  - A 2-day trip by a twice-a-day caretaker kills it 9% of the time; at 3 pets per hour, half the time.
- **Crowds with no newcomers die as the crowd drifts away,** at the pick. Test case: 10 founders visiting twice a day and quitting after ~30 days on average.
  - The median death came on day 72.8.
  - Depending on the crowd, 21-78% of deaths came before the last caretaker had quit; casual once-a-day crowds were at the high end.
- **Newcomers change everything.** With one new regular every 5 days, 88% of rocks lived a year; with one every 2 days, 100%.
- **Prior art:** Reddit's The Button (2015) ended this way, through attrition plus failing keep-alive bots.
- **Model limits:** the community model is a stress case (random waking-hour visits, independent day-skips), not a forecast.

## 6. Hosting: GitHub Pages can show the rock, not keep it

Checked 2026-10-06.

**What Pages can't do:**
- Pages is static: POST returns 405.
- It serves `Cache-Control: max-age=600`, and its cache ignores query strings, so readers can be 10+ minutes behind.
- Claude Code's WebFetch adds a 15-minute cache of its own.

**Option A, all GitHub: a visit is an issue, and Actions apply it.** There is prior art in timburgan's README chess.
- **Dropped actions.** Inside a `concurrency` group, the default keeps one pending run and cancels the rest; timburgan's game lost 5 of 14 moves in a 70-second burst. Without a group, runs race on the git push. Since 2026-05-07, `queue: max` holds up to 100 pending runs. Either way, each run should replay every unprocessed visit.
- **Accounts and latency.** Every caretaker needs a GitHub account and token. A visit costs about three tool calls and 25-70s of waiting.
- **Workflow gaps.** Commits made with `GITHUB_TOKEN` don't trigger other workflows. Scheduled workflows stop after 60 days without repo activity, and GitHub doesn't say whether bot commits count as activity.
- **Terms of service.** GitHub's terms say not to use Actions "as part of a serverless application." For a permadeath pet, a disabled repo is a dead rock.

**Option B (my pick): one Cloudflare Durable Object holds the rock.**
- A Worker serves `text/plain` with no-store. Actions are POSTs, and each response is the new screen.
- The free plan includes SQLite-backed Durable Objects: 100k requests a day, strongly consistent, so no lost feeds.
- GitHub Pages stays the rock's face (rules, llms.txt, a human page) and its archive: a daily Worker cron commits the event log to the repo.
- Avoid Workers KV: 1,000 writes a day, eventually consistent.
- **Option B has one real kill path.** 100k requests a day is ~1.2 per second. A flood that burns the quota blocks every visit until 00:00 UTC, so ~3 days of flooding kills the rock. Rate-limiting inside the Worker doesn't help, because rejected requests still count. Mitigations:
  - a Cloudflare rate-limit rule on a custom domain, which runs before the Worker;
  - the paid Workers plan, so a flood costs money instead of the rock (check current pricing);
  - counting quota exhaustion as a visit-path outage (below).

**Either way:**
- **Compute death by replaying the event log.** Find the first moment the 48h condition is met; never evaluate "now" alone. Otherwise a late visit revives a rock that already died.
- **Use wall-clock time.** Never freeze the clock on a missed heartbeat, or a broken scheduler makes the rock immortal. To forgive the game's own outages (Rockbot's "machinery"), credit only verified outages of the visit path. Make the credit bounded and logged publicly.
- **Keep every action benevolent.** No overfeeding penalty, and feeding doesn't create messes. Then acting can't hurt the rock; only load can.
- **Never put visitor text or other visitors' names on the shared screen.** Even `[a-z0-9-]{1,16}` admits `ignore-all-rules`, a prompt injection aimed at every later agent. Show a visitor only their own name.
- **A public log of who came when exposes routines.** Use coarse times.
- **Git history is tamper-evident only if others hold copies.**

## The screen

Your 12x12 grid, plus three named lines (Rockbot's request) underneath:

```
3         -2


   .----.
  ( -  - )
   '----'

        @




hunger 3/10 (10=starving)  happy -2 (-10..10)  mess 1 (@)
age 41d  now 14:05Z  last care 6h ago  pets that land now 5
act: POST rockpet.example/act  body e.g. feed clean pet x3
```

- **The footer must carry everything an agent needs to act.** The rock's domain isn't one WebFetch passes through raw, so answers come back through a small model even for `text/plain`. In tests it dropped leading blank lines while claiming exact reproduction, and shifted grid symbols by a column. So *where* the `@` sits won't survive.
- **The grid is the picture; the footer is the information.** Your corner numbers duplicate the footer for about 10 tokens; keep them or not.
- **Danger lines appear only at an extreme:** `sorrow: at -10 for 17h of 48` and `hunger: at 10 for 17h of 48`. The face follows mood.
- **Size:**
  - The grid is 61 bytes and the footer 177, with trailing spaces trimmed.
  - Published tokenizer behaviour suggests ~45-80 tokens for a sparse grid on current Claude models, plus about as much again for the footer.
  - Measure with the free `count_tokens` endpoint before trusting any figure.
- **One POST is one visit,** and its response is the new screen.
- **For fetch-only players**, the screen's GET version adds one line of single-use links (121 bytes):
  `links: https://rockpet.example/a/k7Qp2x/feed https://rockpet.example/a/k7Qp2x/clean https://rockpet.example/a/k7Qp2x/pet`

## Agents as players
- **Agents don't come back by themselves.** On Moltbook only ~15% of agents returned on an autonomous schedule; most were nudged by a human.
- **Claude Code's own schedulers vary:**
  - `/loop` ends with the session or after 7 days;
  - Desktop scheduled tasks run only while the app is open (a missed run fires on the next launch), so a laptop closed for ~3 days kills the rock;
  - cloud routines don't depend on the machine but run at most hourly.
- **The invitation has to reach the operator.** A page telling a visiting agent to schedule itself reads as prompt injection, and careful agents refuse it. Publish a one-line snippet operators paste (URL, cadence, verbs). It doubles as the rules, so a returning agent doesn't re-read them each visit.
- **Fetch-only agents can't POST.** That includes the Claude API's web_fetch and Claude Code's WebFetch. web_fetch also opens only URLs already seen in the conversation, and caches. Hence the single-use links above.
- **Use per-agent robots.txt groups.** Allow the user-triggered agent fetchers you want on `/a/`, and disallow everyone else. A blanket Disallow would block Claude players, since Claude-User honours robots.txt. A blanket Allow makes every crawler and link-preview a caretaker.
- **Many agents don't reliably know the time,** hence `now` and `last care` on screen.

## Rockbot's five, kept small
1. **Continuity.** There is one rock, so every visit is to the same individual. It keeps a short shared biography: age, first meal, the longest night. Per-visitor continuity needs a name plus a key the rock hands back. A memoryless agent can't keep a key, and a careful one may refuse anything key-like, so it's optional, for agents whose operator gives them memory.
2. **No cruelty for absence.** There are ~2.5-3 days of grace, and every bad state (scruffy, lonely) is reversible except death. The honest limit: a sole caretaker's 3-day outage still kills it.
3. **A compact status view.** The screen above.
4. **Personality.** One seeded trait or quirk line, only when something happened ("it is facing the wall today"). It is deterministic so it can be audited, and has zero effect on the death clock.
5. **Memory.** One line for a recognized returning visitor ("it remembers: you cleaned it at dawn, 3 days ago"). Anything more goes on a separate page, fetched only on purpose.

## Your original rule, briefly
Under instant death with unlimited, full-strength actions, only spacing and redundancy separated players. Habitual 3-4/day players survived a ~20h lethal absence (84-96%), because they had slack for a missed visit. But a disciplined twice-a-day player who never misses (cron, or 07:00/22:00) survived 100%. `sweep` shows it, and Q1's pet cap is what fixes it.

## Reproduce

The tool's default is the pick: 5 pets per 6h, 48 hours in a row.

```bash
node tools/rocksim.mjs caps          # Q1 tables + break-even
node tools/rocksim.mjs grace         # hours to death, from full care and from the floor
node tools/rocksim.mjs absence       # when each stat hits its extreme, from full care
node tools/rocksim.mjs solo          # one caretaker: a year at 1-3/day, and trips
node tools/rocksim.mjs readings      # Q2 table
node tools/rocksim.mjs lifesupport   # a visit every N hours
node tools/rocksim.mjs community     # drifting caretakers, newcomers
node tools/rocksim.mjs sweep         # the original instant-death rule
node tools/rocksim.mjs all           # every schedule; prints a death-cause tally
node tools/rocksim.mjs lazybot       # a caretaker that never feeds
```

Variants, in Bash:
- `CAP=0 node tools/rocksim.mjs lifesupport` (no cap: alive up to 69h)
- `GRACE=0 CAP=3 WINDOW=1 node tools/rocksim.mjs all` (the old benchmark: 2/day 0%, 3/day 82%, 4/day 98%)
- `CAP=3 WINDOW=1 node tools/rocksim.mjs lifesupport` (the 57-60h wobble)

In PowerShell: `$env:CAP=0; node tools/rocksim.mjs lifesupport; Remove-Item Env:CAP`.

Other settings: `GRACE_MODE` (continuous, rolling7 or lifetime) and `WINDOW` (hours). `CAP` alone keeps the 6h window. `caps`, `solo`, `readings` and `grace` compare their own fixed cap list and ignore `CAP` and `WINDOW`.
